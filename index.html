Salad Mixing Lab

Long-time rod–disk statistics under alternating sine flow in a larger 18 × 12 periodic box.

Model and equations of motion

This is a dimensionless, two-dimensional discrete-element model, not a Navier–Stokes solver. The rods are rigid spherocylinders (capsules), and the lentils are disks. All particles have finite mass, translation and rotation. The external stirring is a prescribed background velocity field; particle contacts can cause deviations from it.

Prescribed alternating sine flow

\[ \mathbf u(\mathbf r,t)= \begin{cases} \left(\dfrac{WK}{\tau}\sin\dfrac{2\pi y}{H},\,0\right), & 0\le t\bmod 2\tau<\tau,\\[6pt] \left(0,\,\dfrac{HK}{\tau}\sin\dfrac{2\pi x}{W}\right), & \tau\le t\bmod 2\tau<2\tau . \end{cases} \]

The box has dimensions W = 18, H = 12 and periodic boundaries. Each half-cycle lasts τ = 2.5 simulation time units. The slider K sets the dimensionless shear displacement. The imposed field is incompressible. Coordinates wrap at opposite boundaries, and particles interact across periodic seams.

Translational motion

\[ m_i\ddot{\mathbf r}_i =\sum_{j\ne i}\mathbf F_{ij}^{\mathrm{contact}} +\gamma_t m_i\bigl[\mathbf u(\mathbf r_i,t)-\dot{\mathbf r}_i\bigr]. \]

The first term sums collisions with both species (rod–rod, disk–disk and rod–disk). The second term relaxes each particle velocity toward the local stirring velocity. In the implementation γₜ = 7, so this is a finite-response model, not tracer advection. No gravity or fluid-mediated interparticle forces are included.

Rotation of the rods

\[ I_i\dot{\omega}_i=\sum_{j\ne i}\tau_{ij}^{\mathrm{contact}} +\gamma_t I_i\left(\Omega_{i,\mathrm{drive}}-\omega_i\right) \] \[ \Omega_{i,\mathrm{drive}}= \frac{\left[\mathbf u(\mathbf r_i+\frac{\ell_i}{2}\mathbf e_i,t) -\mathbf u(\mathbf r_i-\frac{\ell_i}{2}\mathbf e_i,t)\right]\cdot\mathbf e_i^\perp}{\ell_i}. \]

Here eᵢ = (cos θᵢ, sin θᵢ) points along the rod and eᵢ⊥ = (−sin θᵢ, cos θᵢ). This finite-difference approximation drives rod rotation toward the local flow-induced angular velocity. Contact forces also exert torques through their lever arms.

Contact law

\[ \begin{aligned} \delta_{ij}&=a_i+a_j-d_{ij},\\ \mathbf F_n&=\max\!\left(0,k_n\delta_{ij}-\eta_n v_n\right)\hat{\mathbf n}, \qquad(\delta_{ij}>0),\\ \mathbf F_t&=-\mu\,|\mathbf F_n|\tanh\!\left(\frac{v_t}{v_0}\right)\hat{\mathbf t},\\ \tau_{ij}&=(\mathbf r_{\mathrm{contact}}-\mathbf r_i)\times\mathbf F_{ij}. \end{aligned} \]

For rods, dᵢⱼ is computed between their centerline segments (or between a segment and disk center), using the nearest periodic image. Parameters are kₙ = 900, ηₙ = 13 and v₀ = 0.06. This is regularized sliding friction, without history-dependent static friction or permanent binding. Contacts are instantaneous and may appear or disappear during stirring.

Relation to the exact tracer map

\[ \begin{aligned} X_{n+1}&=\left[X_n+K\sin(2\pi Y_n)\right]\bmod 1,\\ Y_{n+1}&=\left[Y_n+K\sin(2\pi X_{n+1})\right]\bmod 1,\\ X&=\frac{x}{W},\qquad Y=\frac{y}{H}. \end{aligned} \]

This exact area-preserving map holds for ideal passive tracers observed once per full cycle. Our rods and disks do not follow it exactly: finite inertia, velocity relaxation and mutual contact forces change their paths. The simulation integrates their equations explicitly with time step Δt = 0.003; velocities are capped as a numerical safeguard.

Observables

Time-averaged disk structure factor Sdd(k)

Averaging starts after t = 10.

The mixing metric is the normalized variance of disk counts in a 4 × 3 spatial grid. For a finite number of randomly distributed disks, it fluctuates above zero. Disk–rod, rod–rod, and disk–disk contacts are enabled.

Each half-period lasts 2.5 time units. In normalized coordinates, passive tracers obey X' = X + K sin(2πY), then Y' = Y + K sin(2πX'), modulo 1.

The rods/disks have inertia, contacts and drag: their trajectories are not exactly this tracer map. Changes take effect on the next run. Use Restart same seed to compare friction.
