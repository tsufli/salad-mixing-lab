Model and equations of motion

This is an exploratory 3D discrete elastic-strip model with no lentils. Each strip is a triangulated rectangular material surface (5 × 2 vertices in the fastest mode, with 5 × 3 and 7 × 3 comparison meshes). Different strips have no permanent bonds. The elastic surface can bend and twist; interpenetration is resisted by finite-thickness, two-sided, soft surface contacts. These are penalty contacts, not an exact guarantee against numerical crossing.

Elastic strip

\[ E_{\mathrm{el}} = E_s+E_b,\qquad E_s=\frac{k_s}{2}\sum_{(a,b)\in\mathcal E} w_{ab}\bigl(\lVert\mathbf r_b-\mathbf r_a\rVert-\ell^0_{ab}\bigr)^2. \]
\[ E_b=\frac{\kappa}{2}\sum_{\alpha}w_\alpha \left\lVert\sum_{a\in\alpha}c_{\alpha a}\mathbf r_a\right\rVert^2. \]

The stretching energy acts on longitudinal, transverse and diagonal mesh edges. The bending penalty uses longitudinal and transverse second differences, as well as mixed differences that penalize twist. The model is invariant to rigid translations and rotations of a flat strip, but is a coarse spring-grid approximation, not a calibrated Kirchhoff–Love shell or a full dihedral-angle shell discretization.

Three-dimensional stirring

\[ \mathbf u(\mathbf r,t)= \begin{cases} \left(\dfrac{LK}{\tau}\sin\dfrac{2\pi y}{L},0,0\right),& 0\leq t\bmod 3\tau\lt\tau,\\[4pt] \left(0,\dfrac{LK}{\tau}\sin\dfrac{2\pi z}{L},0\right),& \tau\leq t\bmod 3\tau\lt2\tau,\\[4pt] \left(0,0,\dfrac{LK}{\tau}\sin\dfrac{2\pi x}{L}\right),& 2\tau\leq t\bmod 3\tau\lt3\tau. \end{cases} \]

Each phase is divergence-free, and the cube is periodic in all three directions, with a user-controlled side length \(L\) (default 7) and \(\tau=2.5\). The field is prescribed: the strips do not feed back onto a fluid. The control \(K\) is the normalized tracer displacement during one shear phase.

Vertex dynamics

\[ m_a\ddot{\mathbf r}_a= -\nabla_a E_{\mathrm{el}} +\sum_{c}\mathbf F_{a,c}^{\mathrm{contact}} +\gamma m_a\left[\mathbf u(\mathbf r_a,t)-\dot{\mathbf r}_a\right]. \]

Contact forces are distributed to the three vertices of each contacting triangle using barycentric weights. We include inter-strip contact and nonlocal self-contact. Initial configurations are rejected if strips have excessively close surfaces.

Frictional surface contact

\[ \begin{aligned} \delta&=\max(0,h-d_{\triangle\triangle}),\\ F_n&=\max(0,k_n\delta-\eta_n v_n),\\ \boldsymbol\xi_t^{\,n+1}&=\operatorname{proj}_{\mathrm{tan}} \left(\boldsymbol\xi_t^{\,n}+\Delta t\,\mathbf v_t\right),\\ \mathbf F_t^{\mathrm{trial}}&=-k_t\boldsymbol\xi_t-\eta_t\mathbf v_t,\qquad \lVert\mathbf F_t\rVert\leq\mu F_n. \end{aligned} \]

The closest points are calculated using vertex–triangle and edge–edge candidates in 3D, with minimum-image periodicity. Tangential displacement history is stored for each contacting triangle pair and reset on separation. The Coulomb cap allows sliding. This does not introduce artificial adhesion or inter-strip bonds.

Network observables

\[ P_\infty(t)=\frac{N_{\mathrm{largest\ connected\ component}}}{N}, \qquad Q(t)=1-\frac{\sum_{i\in C}\lVert\mathbf v'_i-\boldsymbol\omega_*\times\mathbf r'_i\rVert^2}{\sum_{i\in C}\lVert\mathbf v'_i\rVert^2}. \]

The contact graph connects strips with at least one touching triangle pair. Primes mean that cluster translation has been subtracted; \(\boldsymbol\omega_*\) is the best-fit common angular velocity. \(Q\) measures instantaneous rigid-like velocity coherence of strip centers and is undefined for clusters of fewer than three strips. This is not a proof of rigidity, and periodic unwrapping is approximate for very large clusters. We also measure uninterrupted inter-strip contact-pair ages.

Controlled packing before stirring

Instead of removing strips that cannot fit in a dense random placement, all requested strips are first initialized in a larger, dilute periodic cube. Over a chosen number of preparation steps the box contracts smoothly from \(L_0\) to the desired \(L_f\), translating each strip's center without scaling its internal mesh. The strips relax through elastic and frictional forces with stirring disabled. After compression, there is a separate zero-drive relaxation stage at constant box size. Only when relaxation finishes does the alternating sine drive begin, with stirring time starting at \(t=0\). The contact cluster is measured both immediately after compression and after relaxation to test whether it survives without being squeezed.

\[ L(s)=L_0\left(\frac{L_f}{L_0}\right)^{3s^2-2s^3},\qquad s\in[0,1]. \]

Compression is a prescribed geometrical preparation protocol, not a pressure-controlled barostat or real gravity. For density scans, specify the estimated effective volume fraction \(\phi_{\rm eff}=NLwh/L_{\rm box}^{3}\), with the contact thickness used as effective \(h\), and calculate the target cube side length from it. This is not a measured packing fraction or a direct estimate of how much of a real salad bowl is filled. Compression can generate finite soft-contact penetration; monitor the maximum overlap as a fraction of contact thickness.

Numerical limitations

We use a periodic spatial broad phase, finite-thickness spring-dashpot triangle contacts, and semi-implicit Euler integration with \(\Delta t=0.003\). Because contact is soft and there is no continuous collision detection, tunneling or mesh crossing can still occur in difficult geometries. A real claim of topologically faithful entanglement would require collision-safe contact handling, time-step convergence and independent validation. No phase transition is assumed.
